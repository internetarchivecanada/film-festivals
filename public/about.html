About the Film Festival Finder

Film festivals are a big part of how films find audiences, and most of them live on the web: programmes, catalogues, juries, winners. Those pages change every year, and many small festivals disappear altogether. This finder lists every film festival that has an entry on Wikidata, the free knowledge base behind Wikipedia, and shows how well each festival’s website has been kept in the Wayback Machine.

It grew out of the Double Exposure Explorer: to build that, we gathered nine years of the festival’s printed catalogues, and eight of the nine had never been archived. This is the first step toward checking every festival the same way.

What is in it

  • 16,131 festivals: every Wikidata item that is a film festival, or a kind of one (short film festival, documentary film festival, LGBT film festival, and others), as of 17 November 2026. Items that are a single year of another festival are listed on that festival’s page.
  • Country for 10,489: from Wikidata (8,193), from the festival’s place (737), or guessed from a country web address such as .it or .ca (1,559, marked as guessed). 5,642 have no country.
  • Map: 4,498 festivals whose item or place has coordinates.
  • Website for 14,244, from Wikidata’s “official website”.
  • Wayback Machine history: for each website, the years in which its homepage was captured, from the Wayback Machine’s capture index. Checked so far: 114 sites; 114 have captures, 0 have none yet. The check is still running and the site is rebuilt as it goes.

Limits

  • Wikidata is not complete. Big festivals are well described; many small ones have only a name and a website, and many festivals are missing altogether. Wikidata welcomes additions.
  • The homepage history says nothing about the deeper pages (programmes, catalogues, film pages), which are the ones most often missing.
  • “Kind” is a best guess from Wikidata types and from words in the festival’s name.
