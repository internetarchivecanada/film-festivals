About the Film Festival Finder

Film festivals are a big part of how films find audiences, and most of them live on the web: programmes, catalogues, juries, winners. Those pages change every year, and many small festivals disappear altogether. This finder lists every film festival that has an entry on Wikidata, the free knowledge base behind Wikipedia, and shows how well each festival’s website has been kept in the Wayback Machine.

It grew out of the Double Exposure Explorer: to build that, we gathered nine years of the festival’s printed catalogues, and eight of the nine had never been archived. This is the first step toward checking every festival the same way.

What is in it

  • 16,131 festivals: every Wikidata item that is a film festival, or a kind of one (short film festival, documentary film festival, LGBT film festival, and others), as of 17 November 2026. Items that are a single year of another festival are listed on that festival’s page.
  • Country for 10,489: from Wikidata (8,193), from the festival’s place (737), or guessed from a country web address such as .it or .ca (1,559, marked as guessed). 5,642 have no country.
  • Map: 4,498 festivals whose item or place has coordinates.
  • Website for 14,244, from Wikidata’s “official website”. The others have no picture.
  • Wayback Machine history: for each website, the years in which its homepage was captured, from the Wayback Machine’s capture index. Checked on 18 November 2026: 14,230 sites; 14,101 have captures, 129 have none. The check is still running and the site is rebuilt as it goes.

Pictures of the homepages, and saving them

Starting on 19 November 2026 we are visiting every festival website once, in a web browser on a Wayback Machine Labs computer, to take a picture of its homepage, and asking the Wayback Machine’s Save Page Now to archive each homepage, with a screenshot, the same day. Pictures and links appear on the site as they are made; the first pass takes about a day.

  • If a website is up, the picture is the homepage as it looked that day.
  • If it is gone (its address no longer works, it shows an error, or it would not load), or its address now belongs to someone else (a parked or for-sale domain, a server’s default page, or an unrelated site such as an online casino), we show the most recent copy in the Wayback Machine that still looks like the festival’s own site, with its date. Those festivals are gathered under Lost sites.
  • Pictures of unrelated sites that took over a festival’s address are never shown. The check for them is automatic and can miss; please tell us if you see one.
  • Pictures show the top of the page, 1280 by 800 pixels; videos are not loaded, so some show a dark space where a video would play.

Limits

  • Wikidata is not complete. Big festivals are well described; many small ones have only a name and a website, and many festivals are missing altogether. Wikidata welcomes additions.
  • The homepage history says nothing about the deeper pages (programmes, catalogues, film pages), which are the ones most often missing.
  • “Kind” is a best guess from Wikidata types and from words in the festival’s name.

Accessibility

The site is plain HTML with a skip link, labelled navigation and form fields, and enough colour contrast in light and dark mode. The finder’s search and filters and the map need JavaScript; every festival page, and the country and kind lists, work without it. It is meant to meet WCAG 2.2 AA (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines). If something does not work for you, please write to info@archive.org.

Web archives

Web archives are welcome to capture this site. If your crawler has trouble capturing it, contact info@archive.org. There is a sitemap.
